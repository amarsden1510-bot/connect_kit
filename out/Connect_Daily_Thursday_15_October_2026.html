■ Ofsted leadership under fire
▪ NEU snap survey: 98% no confidence. A snap poll of almost 7,000 NEU members in English state schools, run overnight 12-13 October, found 98% have no confidence in Ofsted's current leadership and 96% back an immediate pause on inspections. General secretary Daniel Kebede said Oliver 'has lost the confidence of the profession and it is time for him to go'. (Tes)
▪ Governors: inspections are 'brutal'. The National Governance Association's annual survey found governors describing report-card inspections as 'brutal', with some school leadership teams 'breaking down routinely' under the pressure - despite 71% saying their last inspection gave a fair and accurate picture, up only 3 points on 2022's record low. (Tes)
▪ Second union's no-confidence vote. ASCL's council passed its own no-confidence vote in Ofsted's leadership last week over unaddressed workload and wellbeing concerns. (FE Week)
■ Sheffield & South Yorkshire
▪ Firth Park Academy set for SEND-focused expansion. Sheffield's finance and performance committee meets Monday 19 October to approve passing £602,000 of government funding to Lift Schools, part of a £701,312 scheme adding three SEND rooms, refurbished creative-arts space and upgraded science labs - raising Firth Park's admission number from 240 to 270 to help meet a projected secondary-places shortfall in 2027/28 and 2028/29. (The Star)
■ Pay & funding
▪ Pay policy consultations open from tomorrow. From 16 October, employers can consult staff on pay policies reflecting the accepted 3.5% uplift for 2026-27 (5% at the bottom of the unqualified range), backdated to 1 September. The Pay Order itself still has to clear its parliamentary praying period before arrears follow - worth flagging to business managers as a timing uncertainty. (NASUWT)
