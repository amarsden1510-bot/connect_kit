■ OECD confirms England's teacher exodus
▪ England's teacher attrition rate laid bare in new OECD data. Education at a Glance 2026, published yesterday, shows around 10% of England's teachers left the profession in the latest year measured — over three times the rate in France or Ireland — and more than 90% of those leavers quit voluntarily before retirement, the highest such share in the comparison group. Separate OECD figures show English primary teachers' real-terms pay fell 3.6% between 2015 and 2024. (OECD)
→ For your school: Useful external context for SLT/governors if retention comes up in exit interviews or a staff survey this term.
■ Safeguarding: new duty live today
▪ Information Sharing Duty statutory guidance takes effect today. Under the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026, schools and colleges now have a proactive legal duty to share information relevant to a child's safeguarding or welfare with other agencies, unless sharing would be more harmful than staying silent — aimed at removing the 'better not to share' reflex inquiries have repeatedly flagged. (LGfL safeguarding briefing)
→ For your school: Make sure your DSL has read the guidance and it's referenced in your child protection policy before the next review.
■ Sheffield: the odds of a successful appeal
▪ Nearly 1 in 3 Sheffield secondary appeals succeeded this year — but the rate is falling. Of 358 Sheffield households who appealed a Year 7 place for September 2026, 73 succeeded — a 29.3% success rate, down from 35.3% the year before and well below high-success outliers like Sunderland (88.4%). Figures reported via The Star from official DfE admission appeals statistics. (The Star)
→ For your school: If you support families through admissions, the falling success rate is worth flagging — a strong, well-evidenced first preference matters more this year.
 
Jargon-buster - Information Sharing Duty
New from today under the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026.
A proactive legal duty on schools and named agencies to share safeguarding-relevant information with each other, rather than only responding to requests — unless sharing would cause more harm than staying silent.
