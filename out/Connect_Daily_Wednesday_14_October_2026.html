■ Accountability & Ofsted
▪ ASCL declares no confidence in Ofsted leadership The union's ruling council says it has seen no meaningful progress addressing wellbeing harm from the renewed report-card inspection framework, and wants inspections paused for an urgent review. Members will now be surveyed on a possible industrial-action ballot. (Schools Week)
■ Funding
▪ NEU pushes back on £3.3bn pensions clawback Employer pension contributions fall from 28.6% to 17.6% from April 2027, but DfE has confirmed school funding will be reduced to reflect the saving rather than schools keeping it, unlike a similar local-government pensions saving. NEU general secretary Daniel Kebede argues the money is urgently needed by schools. (Schools Week / Tes)
■ Inclusion & attainment
▪ Phonics results back to pre-pandemic levels - gap claims conflict DfE's 2026 phonics screening check shows 82% of Year 1 pupils met the expected standard, matching 2019 for the first time since the pandemic, with SEN attainment also at its highest since the check began. Commentators flag that more pupils not sitting the check flatters the headline, and reports disagree on whether the disadvantaged-pupil gap (cited variously at 4 or 17 percentage points) has actually narrowed - worth checking DfE's full release before quoting a figure. (Explore Education Statistics (DfE))
■ Sheffield & South Yorkshire
▪ Mayor launches Youth Impact Fund for South Yorkshire's 11-19s Oliver Coppard has launched grants of £2,000-£10,000 for talented young people aged 11-19 across South Yorkshire, covering coaching, equipment, course fees and travel - run with The King's Trust and South Yorkshire's Community Foundation, seeded with £25,000 from the combined authority. Teachers and community leaders can nominate pupils now. (Public Sector Executive)
→ For your school: Consider nominating MAPs pupils who'd benefit.
▪ Sheffield set to expand two secondaries for 2027-29 demand The council's finance and performance committee meets Monday 19 October to approve passing £602,000 of government funding to Firth Park Academy (admission number rising 240 to 270) and a temporary 30-place expansion at Parkwood Academy, ahead of a projected secondary places deficit in 2027/28 and 2028/29. (The Star)
