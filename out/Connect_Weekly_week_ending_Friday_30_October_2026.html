A weekly review of national and South Yorkshire developments in education policy, inclusion, SEND, behaviour and accountability.

 
Top line
▪ Chancellor John Healey delivered his first Autumn Budget on Wednesday 28 October, and it landed light for schools: no new core funding grant, and the OBR's own forecast flags councils' cumulative SEND overspend reaching roughly £5.7bn by 2027-28 (a directional figure, not a confirmed exact one). The two confirmed education lines were a £60m-a-year uplift for PE and school sport partnerships, and up to £5m a year for three years from 2027-28 to get more books to pre-school children.
→ For your school: Don't build budget-planning assumptions on today's commentary — wait for the DfE's own schools-funding breakdown of the settlement before adjusting any forecasts.
▪ The Local Government Association's post-Budget briefing welcomes the already-announced 90% write-off of historic council SEND deficits, but says the Budget gave no clarity on how deficits building up in 2026-27 and 2027-28 will be handled once the Dedicated Schools Grant accounting override ends in 2028. Separately, some councils' write-offs are still being finalised rather than banked.
→ For your school: If your trust works closely with Sheffield City Council on SEND placements or EHCP funding, it's worth asking where the council's own deficit write-off stands before assuming the financial pressure has eased.
▪ The DfE has opened a call for evidence on exclusion reform, covering a time-limited internal suspension framework, formal reintegration-support partnerships after a suspension, and the day-one schoolwork duty flagged in last week's edition. It closes 19 November.
→ For your school: Worth a look from whoever leads your behaviour policy — this is the detail stage of reforms that could reshape how suspensions and reintegration work, and responding now is the way to influence the final shape.
▪ A genuinely quiet week nationally for Ofsted and safeguarding specifically, and Sheffield schools have been shut for half term since Monday — so this edition is lighter than usual on those two beats rather than padded out.
→ For your school: No action needed — a reasonable week to catch up on the exclusion reform call for evidence or the Budget detail instead.

■ National policy
▪ Budget lands light for schools, with the SEND funding gap the real story. Chancellor John Healey's first Budget included few schools-specific announcements beyond the £60m-a-year PE and school sport uplift and the £15m pre-school books commitment. The bigger signal was the OBR's own forecast of a widening council SEND deficit, and commentary that mainstream per-pupil funding is on track to fall in real terms as funding is held flat against falling pupil numbers, with no new grant confirmed to cover schools' rising minimum-wage costs. It's the first full fiscal event under Education Secretary Lucy Powell, who took over from Bridget Phillipson in the summer reshuffle that also brought in Healey as Chancellor. (Tes)
→ For your school: Hold off on budget-planning assumptions until the DfE publishes its own schools-funding breakdown of this settlement, expected in the coming weeks.
■ Funding
▪ SEND deficit write-off welcomed, but the next two years are still unresolved. The LGA's Autumn Budget briefing says councils welcomed the previously-announced 90% write-off of historic Dedicated Schools Grant deficits, but is disappointed there's no clarity in this Budget on how deficits accruing in 2026-27 and 2027-28 will be addressed before the accounting override ends in 2028. Separately, councils are reportedly unhappy that surpluses in other DSG blocks will offset their deficits in practice, meaning some will see less than a full 90% write-off — and a number of councils won't have their deficits written off this autumn at all because their SEND reform plans need more work. It's the same structural problem the Budget was meant to start answering, and didn't. (Local Government Association)
→ For your school: Worth checking directly with Sheffield City Council's finance team where its own write-off and SEND reform plan stand, rather than assuming national coverage reflects the local position.
■ Inclusion, SEND & behaviour
▪ Exclusion reform moves from consultation to call for evidence. The DfE has opened a formal call for evidence on three proposals trailed in the Schools White Paper: a time-limited internal suspension framework that keeps pupils on-site rather than sending them home, formal reintegration-support partnerships with an agreed plan after a suspension, and the day-one schoolwork duty covered in last week's edition. It closes 19 November. A charity response from Contact has already flagged that most exclusions are linked to needs not being properly supported, and says it will push for the reforms to work for children with SEND specifically — a reminder that behaviour and SEND policy are, in practice, the same conversation. (GOV.UK)
→ For your school: If your school or trust has a view on internal suspension or reintegration practice, this is the window to feed it in before the policy hardens — treat current proposals as indicative, not settled.

Nothing new and reliably dated on safeguarding policy surfaced this week beyond the ongoing NEU formal strike ballot over pay, funding and workload, which closes 15 December and sits as much in the wellbeing and workload space as in industrial relations (see diary). KCSIE 2026 remains in effect since 1 September with no amendments reported. (NEU)
→ For your school: Worth keeping half an eye on the NEU ballot outcome in December, given the direct read-across to staff workload and wellbeing conversations in your own setting.
■ Sheffield & South Yorkshire
▪ A strong set of local Ofsted results, but half term keeps the wider picture quiet. The Star's latest round-up reports ten Sheffield schools and nurseries praised in reports published across September and October, including High Storrs School's top grade in all seven areas and Bright Horizons Millhouses Day Nursery matching it. Beyond that, the city's schools have been closed for the autumn half term since Monday 26 October, returning 2 November, so no new city-level education story broke this week. Last week's unresolved Firth Park Academy funding decision still has no published outcome in our searches. (The Star)
→ For your school: If you need the Firth Park funding outcome, it's still worth checking directly with the council rather than waiting for it to surface in press coverage.
