Top line
▪ Ofsted's annual refresh of the inspection toolkit and operating guide for 2026/27 is now in effect, covering early years, schools, independent schools, FE & skills and ITE providers.
→ For your school: Skim the schools-specific changes now rather than waiting for your inspection window.

→ For your school: Confirm your DSL has reviewed the new information-sharing and AI-safeguarding provisions against your current policy.

→ For your school: Get any school or trust response in before the deadline; both feed directly into the Schools White Paper reforms.

→ For your school: Ask your local area what its Experts at Hand offer will look like and when your school can expect access.

■ Ofsted & accountability
▪ September 2026 inspection toolkit and operating guide now in effect. Ofsted's annual update to its inspection materials for the 2026/27 cycle covers early years settings, state-funded schools, non-association independent schools, FE & skills providers and initial teacher education. It builds on the report-card framework introduced in November 2025 — a five-point scale (exceptional, strong standard, expected standard, needs attention, urgent improvement), with safeguarding judged separately as met or not met — and sharpens focus on outcomes for pupils with 'barriers to their learning or wellbeing'. (Ofsted)
→ For your school: Check the updated toolkit's treatment of pupils with barriers to learning — it's now an explicit line of inspection enquiry.
■ Safeguarding & wellbeing
▪ KCSIE 2026 in force from 1 September. The updated statutory guidance introduces an information-sharing duty stemming from the Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026, requiring safeguarding organisations and practitioners to share relevant information; schools will also get notifications on domestic abuse incidents and pupils in temporary accommodation in some circumstances. Separately, the Crime and Policing Act 2026 removes the 'supervision exemption', so anyone teaching, training or supervising children is now in regulated activity regardless of supervision. New provisions also cover generative AI use and updated online-risk categories. (GOV.UK / NSPCC)
→ For your school: Check your safer-recruitment checks reflect the regulated-activity change — this affects volunteers and unqualified staff who supervise pupils.
■ Inclusion & SEND
▪ Two funding/reform consultations close 18 September. The EOTAS consultation covers oversight and quality assurance of education arranged outside school for children with EHC plans; the linked 'local SEND inclusion formula' consultation, open since 10 July, tests giving mainstream schools more SEND funding upfront rather than via high-needs top-ups, from the 2027/28 financial year. (GOV.UK)
▪ 'Experts at Hand' service begins rolling out. Local areas are starting to expand access to speech and language therapists, occupational therapists, specialist teachers and educational psychologists working directly alongside mainstream schools, early years settings and colleges — backed by roughly £1.8bn over three years as part of the Schools White Paper reforms. (GOV.UK)
→ For your school: Ask your local area for its rollout timeline and how to request support for a specific pupil or cohort.
■ Funding
▪ Free school meals expansion now in effect. From this September, FSM eligibility extends to all households receiving Universal Credit, regardless of earnings; schools should expect eligibility-recheck activity through the term as transitional protections from the previous system unwind. (GOV.UK)
▪ Inclusive Mainstream Fund's first year. The fund is worth over £500m a year, with roughly £400m going directly to mainstream schools for primary and secondary provision — 2026/27 is its first year of operation. (GOV.UK)
▪ Academy funding agreements no longer published centrally. From this September the department has stopped publishing academy funding agreements on GOV.UK; trusts must now publish redacted versions on their own websites — worth checking your trust's site reflects this. (Tes)
→ For your school: If you're a trust comms lead, confirm your redacted funding agreement is live on your own website.
■ National policy
▪ DfE Update, 9 September 2026. The department's regular fortnightly bulletin to schools, trusts and local authorities was published this week; these updates typically carry grant deadlines, data-collection windows and operational guidance rather than headline policy, so worth a scan for anything time-limited. (GOV.UK)
■ Sheffield & South Yorkshire
▪ Body-worn cameras for Sheffield's school crossing patrols. Sheffield City Council is equipping lollipop patrol wardens at several crossings with body-worn cameras, following incidents of abuse from drivers, to protect staff and support safer routes to school. (The Star)
▪ Rotherham Schools Forum meets 18 September. The agenda includes the exclusion & suspensions annual report, an EHCP update and early 2027/28 budget planning — a useful read-across given Sheffield's own high-needs budget pressures. (Rotherham MBC)
