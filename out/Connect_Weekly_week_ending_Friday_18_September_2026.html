■ National policy
▪ First overhaul of School Food Standards in over a decade. From 14 September the DfE set out new statutory food standards for schools: deep-fried food is banned, processed meat and cakes/sweet treats are capped (cheese as a main ingredient no more than twice a week, for example), and schools must serve more fruit, vegetables and fibre. The changes are subject to parliamentary approval and won't take effect until September 2027, with secondary-specific measures phased in by 2028 — giving catering teams a genuine lead-in. It follows this year's wider expansion of free school meals to all Universal Credit households, so schools serving more free meals will also, in time, be serving different ones. (GOV.UK)
→ For your school: Share the implementation timeline with your catering provider or trust now — 2027 sounds distant but supplier contracts and menu redesign take lead time.
■ Funding
▪ £500m pay-funding breakthrough averts threatened strike. After weeks of NEU pressure over the 3.5% September pay award, the DfE has told the union the increase will be fully funded after all — via roughly £500m freed up by a 4.9 percentage-point fall in employers' local-government pension contributions, money schools keep rather than have clawed back centrally. The NEU called it 'a fully funded pay increase for all teachers' and its national executive meets on 24 September to decide on the strike ballot due to open 3 October. (Schools Week)
→ For your school: Hold off finalising any in-year budget virement for the pay award until the 24 September decision is confirmed.
▪ FSM autumn-census deadline pushed back a month. The DfE has extended the deadline for completing free-school-meals eligibility checks for the autumn census from 1 October to 2 November — a one-off extension to support the wider FSM/Universal Credit expansion rollout flagged in last issue. (GOV.UK (DfE Update, 16 September 2026))
→ For your school: If your eligibility-check backlog was tight, this buys a useful extra month.
■ Inclusion & SEND
▪ Twin SEND funding/reform consultations closed today. The EOTAS consultation covers oversight and quality assurance of education arranged outside school for children with EHC plans; the linked 'local SEND inclusion formula' consultation would let local authorities raise the £6,000 SEND top-up threshold — unchanged since 2013 — via schools forum agreement. Both closed 18 September with no outcome yet; both would apply from April 2027 if taken forward, so expect analysis in the coming weeks rather than a result this issue. (GOV.UK)
→ For your school: Watch for the government response — a raised top-up threshold would directly change what counts as 'high needs' funding for your SEND budget.
■ Behaviour, exclusions & attendance
▪ Suspension rates keep falling, but the gap doesn't. FFT Education Datalab's latest analysis of 2025/26 attendance data (drawn from around 9,000 subscribing schools) finds the combined suspension-and-exclusion rate fell from 4.1% to 3.8% in autumn term, extending the post-pandemic decline seen in earlier years — though primary years 1-4 saw a small rise. Disadvantaged pupils remain far more likely to be excluded or suspended than their peers, a pattern echoed this week by Stocksbridge High's Ofsted finding (see Sheffield & South Yorkshire, below). Figures are FFT's own analysis, provisional pending the DfE's official autumn release. (FFT Education Datalab)
→ For your school: A useful benchmark if disadvantage-gap language is going into this year's SEF.

■ Sheffield & South Yorkshire
▪ Nine Sheffield schools and nurseries get new Ofsted reports. The Star's latest round-up covers reports published over the summer and into the first week of term. Swallownest Primary was praised across the board, with inspectors noting pupils 'arrive happily and settle quickly'; Bankwood Community recovered from 'Inadequate' in 2022 to meeting the expected standard in all areas. Stocksbridge High fared worse — two areas rated 'needs urgent improvement' and four 'needs attention', with inspectors flagging 'unacceptable' attendance and disadvantaged pupils leaving without basic qualifications. (The Star)
→ For your school: Stocksbridge's report is a live local example of this week's disadvantage-gap and attendance data — worth a look if you're benchmarking your own inclusion offer.

Jargon-buster
This week's twin SEND consultations turn on one acronym.
EOTAS — education otherwise than at school — is provision a local authority arranges for a child with an EHC plan who, for reasons such as ongoing ill health, isn't attending a school placement. This week's consultation looked at how councils oversee and quality-assure it.
