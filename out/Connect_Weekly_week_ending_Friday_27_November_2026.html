Top line
▪ The dominant story for schools this fortnight is the Milburn review of youth unemployment, published 17 November: a 277-page final report commissioned by the Work and Pensions Secretary that sets a headline target of cutting the NEET rate from 13% to around 5% by 2036, and explicitly blames schools being judged on qualifications rather than destinations as part of the problem.
→ For your school: Expect post-16 destination data and careers provision to move up the accountability agenda — worth reviewing your own destinations tracking now, ahead of any formal policy response.
▪ DfE's own autumn term 2025/26 suspensions and exclusions statistics, published 19 November, show both measures falling — suspensions down 7% to around 312,000 and permanent exclusions down 12% to around 3,300 — which cuts against some of the 'record high' framing that's circulated this year, most of it referring to older or full-year data rather than this release.
→ For your school: If you're using national exclusion data in a governors' report or benchmarking exercise, check you're citing this autumn-term release and not an older or misremembered figure.
▪ Two safeguarding threads landed close together: DfE quietly clarified on 17 November that most work-experience placements don't need a DBS check, while separately two multi-academy trusts (Oasis, 59 schools; GLF, 43 schools) withdrew David Walliams's books from library shelves pending unproven misconduct allegations, with ASCL now asking DfE for national guidance rather than leaving individual schools to decide.
→ For your school: Hold off changing library stock unilaterally — worth waiting for ASCL/DfE guidance given the allegations are contested and the trust decisions are precautionary, not findings of fact.
▪ Locally, Sheffield had two live threads this week: a mass student walkout on 20 November over defence spending that drew in High Storrs, King Edward VII and Dronfield Henry Fanshawe pupils, and the continuing NEU strike at Talbot Specialist School, the council-maintained SEND school in Norton Lees, where further strike days are running through the rest of November.
→ For your school: If your catchment overlaps with the schools named in the walkout coverage, it's worth checking how your own attendance and safeguarding response compared on the day.

■ National policy & accountability
▪ Milburn's final NEET report lands with a steep target and a direct critique of school accountability. The Department for Work and Pensions published Alan Milburn's final report, 'From a welfare state to a working state', on 17 November — following his interim report in May — setting a target of cutting England's NEET rate from 13% to around 5% by 2036. Among its schools-facing recommendations: primary schools identifying pupils at risk of becoming NEET far earlier, work experience becoming a statutory duty rather than, as the review puts it, 'a statutory duty without enforcement', and schools held accountable for what happens to pupils after they leave rather than just the qualifications they leave with. Early commentary from the IFS and the Institute for Government is sceptical about deliverability — the recommendations span several government departments and tiers of government, and nothing here is funded or legislated yet. (GOV.UK)
→ For your school: Nothing to action immediately since this is a recommendations document, not policy — but post-16 destinations tracking and careers provision are likely to get more scrutiny, so it's worth knowing where your own data stands.
■ Behaviour & attendance
▪ Official exclusions data shows suspensions and permanent exclusions both falling, not rising. DfE's autumn term 2025/26 release, published 19 November, shows the suspension rate falling to 3.77 per 10,000 pupils from 4.02 a year earlier — around 312,000 suspensions, a 7% drop from roughly 336,000. Permanent exclusions fell further, down 12% to around 3,300 from about 3,700, with the rate holding at 0.04. Persistent disruptive behaviour remains by far the most common reason cited for suspension. This is worth flagging because some sector commentary this year has described suspensions as being at a 'record' or 'six-year high' — that framing appears to trace back to a different (likely full academic year 2024/25) dataset rather than this autumn-term release, so treat any such claim with caution until you've checked which release it's citing. (GOV.UK / Explore Education Statistics)
→ For your school: Worth double-checking which release any 'record high' claim you see is based on before using it in a governors' paper — this one shows the opposite direction.
■ Funding
▪ Schools operational guide update folds the schools budget support grant into the national funding formula from 2027/28. DfE's Schools Operational Guide for 2027 to 2028, updated 19 November, confirms the schools budget support grant (SBSG) — introduced as a top-up in 2026/27 — is being rolled into the national funding formula rather than continuing as a separate grant. That's a technical change rather than a funding cut or boost in itself, but it will affect how individual schools see their allocation presented from 2027/28, and finance teams will want to understand the mechanics before the next budget-setting cycle rather than after allocations land. It sits alongside the IFS's broader forecast of a roughly 9% real-terms rise in per-pupil spending between 2025/26 and 2028/29, which is the most optimistic funding outlook the sector has had in some years. (GOV.UK)
→ For your school: Finance leads should read the updated operational guide now rather than wait for the 2027/28 allocation letters, since the SBSG-into-NFF change affects how your budget line will be presented.
■ Safeguarding & wellbeing
▪ DfE clarifies that most work-experience placements don't need a DBS check. Guidance published on GOV.UK on 17 November confirms that employers don't need a DBS check — including an enhanced certificate — to offer work experience to pupils aged 16 and over under normal supervision, and that family members supervising a placement don't need one either. The position for under-16 placements is less clean: barred list checks may still be required where a child under 16 is being supervised, and legal commentary (Browne Jacobson, The Key) notes the guidance doesn't say much about how schools should evidence that the exemption conditions are actually met. Tes reports school leaders remain concerned this ambiguity could discourage smaller employers from offering placements at all. (GOV.UK)
→ For your school: Safeguarding leads reviewing work-experience policy this term should check it against this guidance directly, particularly for any under-16 placements, rather than relying on last year's position.
▪ Trusts pull David Walliams's books from library shelves as ASCL seeks DfE guidance. Oasis Community Learning (59 schools) and GLF Schools (43 schools) have temporarily withdrawn David Walliams's books from their school libraries following misconduct allegations the author denies. ASCL has asked DfE for national guidance rather than leaving the decision to individual schools, and the response elsewhere is inconsistent — Kent County Council has made no change to its public libraries, while Camden has moved copies into storage. It's worth being clear that these are precautionary decisions by individual institutions, not findings against Walliams, and no formal process has concluded. (NationalWorld)
→ For your school: If you're being asked by staff or parents what your school's position is, it's reasonable to say you're waiting for ASCL/DfE guidance rather than making a unilateral call on unproven allegations.
■ Sheffield & South Yorkshire
▪ Mass student walkout over defence spending draws in several Sheffield secondaries. Students at High Storrs School, King Edward VII School and Dronfield Henry Fanshawe School were among those expected to join a Socialist Students-organised walkout in Sheffield city centre on Friday 20 November, opposing the government's defence budget increase; Sheffield College and both city universities were also involved. High Storrs wrote to parents beforehand saying it would 'strongly prefer' pupils to stay in lessons, citing safety concerns, while South Yorkshire Police said they were planning an increased city-centre presence for the day. We haven't seen confirmed turnout figures or reports of disruption from the day itself. (The Star)
→ For your school: Worth checking with pastoral staff how your own attendance and any parental correspondence around the 20th compared with neighbouring schools named in the coverage.
▪ Strike action continues at Talbot Specialist School. NEU members at Talbot Specialist School, the council-maintained SEND school in Norton Lees, walked out on 5 November in a dispute over workload, safety and staffing levels, with the union citing a failure by the school to engage in meaningful talks. Five further strike days were planned across the rest of November, which means the action is continuing through this week; we haven't seen reporting confirming the dispute has been resolved. Unlike most Sheffield secondaries, Talbot sits under direct council control rather than an academy trust, which keeps the dispute squarely with the council rather than a trust. (The Star)
→ For your school: Worth watching if your trust has SEND provision staffed similarly, since workload and safety — not pay — are the specific grounds cited.
