■ National policy & funding
▪ All eyes on this afternoon's Budget. Chancellor John Healey - who took the role in July when Andy Burnham became Prime Minister - delivers his first Budget today, with the Office for Budget Responsibility's forecast published alongside it. He has so far declined to confirm or rule out tax rises, and analysts think the fiscal headroom built up by his predecessor has likely shrunk on weaker growth and higher borrowing costs. Nothing is confirmed yet on mainstream or high-needs school funding. (LBC)

▪ NEU ballot still running. The NEU's formal strike ballot over pay, funding and workload - covering teachers and support staff separately - remains open and closes 15 December. In the earlier indicative ballot, 96% of responding teachers backed rejecting the pay offer and said they'd be prepared to take action, though turnout was under 50%. (NEU)
 
Jargon-buster - fiscal headroom
Today's Budget speculation keeps coming back to this phrase.
"Fiscal headroom" is the gap between what the Chancellor plans to spend or borrow and the limit set by the government's own fiscal rules, as judged by OBR forecasts. Less headroom this time around means less room for new spending promises - schools included - without tax rises or cuts elsewhere.
