■ Funding
▪ DfE confirms £2.6bn pension clawback from school budgets. Schools Week's 19 November report confirms what had been trailed as a possibility a fortnight earlier: the government will claw back £2.6bn from school budgets to reflect the Teachers' Pension Scheme's employer contribution rate falling from 28.6% to 17.6% from April 2027. The DfE's own framing is that schools' net spending capacity will still rise by about £1.7bn nationally once other funding changes are counted in - but that nets the clawback off against separate growth elsewhere, rather than letting schools bank the pension saving itself. It lands differently from September's decision not to claw back a separate, smaller saving schools made on support-staff pensions through the Local Government Pension Scheme, which schools were allowed to keep in full. (Schools Week)
→ For your school: Build the clawback into medium-term budget modelling now - finance leads shouldn't treat the 2027 contribution fall as cash schools will actually see.
■ SEND, inclusion & alternative provision
▪ Another quiet week for SEND reform nationally - the funding question still has no date attached. Nothing new has moved on SEND system reform since the Education Secretary told MPs that the £6bn shortfall identified by the Office for Budget Responsibility is 'a matter for the next spending review', not something to resolve now - a position that leaves schools managing current pressures without a funding answer for at least another financial year. The OBR's own forecast puts the gap at £6bn by 2028-29, rising to £9bn by 2030-31; the department has privately pushed back on how that forecast is being presented, without disputing that a shortfall exists in some form. (Schools Week)
→ For your school: If SEND budget pressures are already acute in your setting, treat this as a signal to plan for at least one more year without new national funding, not a reason to expect a near-term fix.
■ Behaviour, exclusions & attendance
▪ Autumn exclusion statistics overdue; breakfast club funding window now open. The DfE's accredited autumn 2025/26 suspensions and permanent exclusions statistics were expected 'around now' - last year's equivalent landed on 20 November - but hadn't appeared on Explore Education Statistics as we went to press, so the first official read on this autumn's behaviour data remains pending. In the meantime, the monitoring form covering the 9-13 November breakfast clubs census week opened on 16 November and closes 4 December; DfE guidance ties per-pupil funding under the programme to average recorded attendance, so a late submission costs a term's payment. (GOV.UK (Explore Education Statistics))
→ For your school: Keep checking Explore Education Statistics for the exclusions release, and confirm this week that breakfast club registers are complete ahead of the 4 December deadline.
■ Safeguarding & wellbeing
▪ Anti-Bullying Week 2026 concludes today under the theme 'Break the Silence'. The Anti-Bullying Alliance's campaign week, coordinated by the National Children's Bureau, ran 16-20 November highlighting children who experience bullying without telling anyone; the NCB puts the campaign's estimated annual reach at around 8 million people across the UK. There's no new DfE guidance attached this year, so it remains a pastoral and assembly-planning moment rather than a policy one - though it sits naturally alongside KCSIE 2026's rewritten mental health and wellbeing expectations, which already ask schools to take a more whole-school approach to emotional wellbeing. (National Children's Bureau)
→ For your school: If assembly or PSHE materials haven't gone out yet, today's the last day of the week itself - worth a swift wrap-up message to pastoral teams either way.
■ Workforce, pay & industrial action
▪ NEU's formal strike ballot moves closer to its mid-December close. The NEU's formal ballot of teacher and support-staff members over pay, funding and workload remains open and closes 15 December. It sits in the same frame as this week's other workforce stories - the pension funding row above and the Talbot Specialist School dispute below - since pay, funding and workload are the common threads running through all three. Any formal mandate for action arising from the ballot would land in the new year, after schools have had to absorb the detail of the pension clawback into their own budget planning. (NEU)
→ For your school: Keep this on the radar for January planning - a formal mandate, if secured, would be the first national action stemming from this year's combined pay, funding and workload grievance.
■ Sheffield & South Yorkshire
▪ Council approves ~£2.14m to expand two secondary schools. Sheffield City Council's finance and performance policy committee approved funding on 16 November to temporarily expand Hinde House School in Shiregreen by 90 places and extend Forge Valley School in Stannington; Forge Valley's DfE-funded budget was trimmed by £171,600 to £4.53m during the same meeting. Both projects are due to complete by August 2027, and this is the clearest confirmation yet of how the council intends to meet the secondary-place shortfall it has been forecasting through to 2029. (The Star)
→ For your school: North Sheffield secondary capacity continues to be the council's main lever against the 2029 shortfall forecast - worth tracking if your catchment overlaps.
▪ Strike action continues at Talbot Specialist School, with no resolution reported. NEU members at Talbot Specialist School, the council-maintained SEND school in Norton Lees, have continued strike action through this fortnight as part of six strike days called in November over workload, safety and governance concerns; Sheffield City Council has asked the union and its members to reconsider the action. The dispute has a longer history than this month's headlines suggest - a 2023 plan for Talbot to join a multi-academy trust was paused after the NEU raised 'major concerns' that the trust's culture wasn't suited to a specialist setting, and governance transparency is cited again in this dispute. (The Star)
→ For your school: Worth watching if your trust has specialist SEND provision with similar staffing models - workload and governance, not pay, are the stated grounds here.
