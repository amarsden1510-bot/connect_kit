■ National policy
▪ Powell unveils vocational GCSEs at Labour conference. Closing Labour's conference in Liverpool (27-30 September), education secretary Lucy Powell confirmed plans for new vocational GCSEs — covering subjects such as business management, child development and construction — to replace existing technical awards from 2029, sitting alongside A-levels, T-levels and the new V-levels in a reshaped 16-19 progression landscape. Powell framed it as ending ‘the snobbery towards technical education’, a message aimed as much at her own party as at schools. (Schools Week (Labour conference live blog))
→ For your school: Nothing to action given the 2029 start date, but worth flagging to careers leads and Year 9 options teams as an early planning signal.
▪ NFER warns the new GCSEs could widen inequality if take-up is patchy. Responding within days of the conference announcement, NFER's Jude Hillary cautioned that international evidence shows earlier academic/vocational tracking tends to entrench rather than narrow socio-economic gaps, since subject choices at 14 often reflect prior attainment and family background as much as aptitude. The government insists it isn't formally splitting pupils into two tracks — but NFER's point is that uneven take-up could do exactly that in practice. (Schools Week)
→ For your school: A useful line to have ready if parents or governors ask whether the new qualifications are a genuine alternative route or a lower-status one by another name.
■ Funding
▪ Autumn school census opened Thursday 1 October. This year's return — which feeds directly into funding allocations including the National Funding Formula and growth funding calculations — opened on 1 October, after the DfE's final familiarisation window for checking data closed on 25 September. Local authority return deadlines vary, so don't assume the DfE's own later cut-off applies to your school. (GOV.UK (DfE Update academies, 30 September 2026))
→ For your school: If this hasn't already gone through your usual data-checking process, chase whoever holds it today — errors here flow straight into next year's budget.
▪ Two further funding deadlines from this week's DfE Update. The same 30 September update confirmed the quarter 2 update to 2026-27 pupil premium allocations, with payment due to academies on Thursday 8 October, and opened funding claim forms for the Care to Learn and 16-19 Bursary Fund's defined vulnerable groups element, due by 5pm on Wednesday 11 November. (GOV.UK (DfE Update academies, 30 September 2026))
→ For your school: Both dates are now in the diary below — diarise them locally too if either funding stream applies to your setting.
■ Inclusion & SEND
▪ DfE tests draft guidance on 'reasonable' SEND adjustments for mainstream schools. As part of the wider SEND reform package, the DfE has shared draft examples with trust leaders of what counts as a reasonable — and unreasonable — adjustment for pupils with additional needs: for instance, a supervised early-arrival window and access to a low-stimulation space for a pupil whose sensory processing needs affect attendance. It's a practical companion to the Code of Practice revision promised in the schools white paper, and lands the same week Ofsted's own inclusion grading area (see below) continues to bed in, so expect the two to be read together by inspectors and schools alike. (Tes)
→ For your school: Worth getting in front of your SENDCo now — these examples will likely shape what Ofsted treats as 'reasonable' once finalised.
■ Behaviour, exclusions & attendance
▪ A quiet week for English data — but Wales offers a stark illustration. Neath Port Talbot council fined 159 parents and took 48 to court over school absence in 2025/26, while 638 pupils were excluded — persistent disruptive behaviour the leading cause, and the exclusion rate among girls now nearly double its 2018/19 level. No comparable English national dataset landed this week, but the gendered shift mirrors a pattern researchers have also flagged in English figures. (Swansea Bay News)
→ For your school: If your own behaviour data shows a similar drift in who's being excluded, it's worth raising internally before it surfaces in an inspection.
■ Ofsted & accountability
▪ Powell signals Ofsted changes 'within the next few months'. Following NAHT's survey of over 5,000 leaders — nearly three-quarters of whom said inspection had got more stressful under the renewed framework, with the union demanding a pause on graded inspections over fears for leaders' health — Powell told Schools Week she's planning changes to make the accountability system 'more supportive and understanding'. NAHT's general secretary called a timeframe of 'months rather than weeks' a 'problem', so expect continued pressure before anything concrete lands. (Schools Week)
→ For your school: Not actionable yet, but worth watching — any framework change would affect how you prepare for inspection from next term.
■ Safeguarding & wellbeing
▪ Education secretary unveils whole-school plan to tackle misogyny. Announced 26 September, the plan gives schools practical guides, teacher training and parent-support resources to counter the influence of figures like Andrew Tate, backed by a new £150,000 RSHE expert advisory group and two programmes launched under the £16m Violence Against Women and Girls Prevention Programme. It follows Ofsted's own observation of a 'growing scourge' of classroom misogyny, linking this week's safeguarding announcement to the accountability conversation above. (GOV.UK)
→ For your school: Check whether your RSHE lead has seen the new guides yet — they're designed to be used rather than filed.
■ Sheffield & South Yorkshire
▪ Rotherham reviews school-gate parking safety after dangerous-driving complaints. A scrutiny review — going to Rotherham's Overview and Scrutiny Management Board on 7 October — found just one council CCTV enforcement van currently covers more than 200 school access points, with parents reporting dangerous parking, blocked driveways and near-misses at drop-off and pick-up. Councillor Adam Tinsley, who chaired the review, is pushing for stronger enforcement action. (Rotherham Advertiser)
→ For your school: If this resonates, it's worth checking whether your own travel plan addresses drop-off congestion before a similar review lands locally.
▪ Five of Rotherham's 16 secondaries are oversubscribed on first choice. New analysis of government data shows five of Rotherham's 16 secondary schools received more first-choice applications than places available, with St Bernard's Catholic High School the most oversubscribed — 254 first-choice applications for 146 places. It's a reminder that place pressure isn't only a Sheffield city story; it sits alongside this week's autumn census (above) as another data point local authorities will be watching closely this term. (Rotherham Advertiser)
→ For your school: Useful context if parents raise oversubscription concerns with you ahead of secondary application season.
 
Jargon-buster — V-level
Comes up in this week's vocational GCSEs story (National policy, above).
A new post-16 qualification sitting alongside A-levels and T-levels, aimed at 16-19 learners wanting a mix of classroom and applied vocational study. The new vocational GCSEs announced this week are pitched as the 14-16 on-ramp into this three-track system — so what you teach pre-16 will increasingly map onto one of the three routes.
