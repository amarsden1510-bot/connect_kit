■ National policy & funding
▪ Teachers' pay rise now fully funded, DfE confirms. Education secretary Lucy Powell told the NEU that recent local government pension scheme revaluations free up roughly £500m for schools this year, meaning the pay award won't need to come out of existing budgets after all. The NEU's national executive meets on 24 September to decide whether to stand down its planned ballot. (Schools Week)
→ For your school: Worth holding off on any budget-planning adjustments until the 24 September decision is confirmed.
■ Behaviour & attendance
▪ Suspension rates keep falling — but the disadvantage gap doesn't. New FFT Education Datalab analysis of autumn term 2025/26 finds secondary suspension and exclusion rates still falling, extending last year's post-pandemic reversal, while primary years 1-4 saw a small rise. Disadvantaged pupils remain far more likely than their peers to be excluded or suspended. Figures are FFT's own analysis, flagged here as provisional pending the DfE's official autumn release. (FFT Education Datalab)
→ For your school: Worth checking against your own autumn-term figures if disadvantage-gap language is going into this year's SEF.
■ Sheffield & South Yorkshire
▪ Newfield's first Ofsted since the Mercia takeover: a turnaround story. Inspectors called Newfield Secondary (Norton Lees) 'warm, caring, calm, orderly and respectful' in its first report since Mercia Learning Trust took over in 2025, rating attendance/behaviour and inclusion at 'strong standard' — a marked shift after last year's contentious takeover, which included a pupil walkout over proposed arts cuts. (The Star)
→ For your school: A live example of inclusion recovering post-MAT-transition — useful if you're advising other schools going through the same.
