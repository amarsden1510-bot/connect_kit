■ Ofsted & accountability
▪ Antisemitism review lands with 27 accepted recommendations — and a new Ofsted remit. Sir David Bell's independent review, launched after Bristol Brunel Academy postponed a visit from Jewish MP Damien Egan earlier this year, reported on 21 October with 27 recommendations covering leader training and how schools respond to external campaigners. Government has accepted all of them in full. Among the changes: Ofsted and the Independent Schools Inspectorate will carry out thematic scrutiny of how academy trusts implement and oversee antisemitism policy, rather than leaving it solely to individual school inspections. It lands only a fortnight after ASCL and NAHT's no-confidence motions in Ofsted's leadership over the report-card framework's wellbeing toll — handing Ofsted a new, specific accountability role at the same moment its general capacity and credibility are under the heaviest scrutiny in years. (Schools Week)
→ For your school: Worth a line in your next safeguarding/EDI self-evaluation on how your trust (not just your school) records and responds to discriminatory incidents, since that trust-level view is now explicitly inspectable.
■ National policy
▪ DfE's top civil servant departs as multiple reform threads stay live. Susan Acland-Hood is stepping down as DfE permanent secretary after six years in the post; Simon Ridley, currently second permanent secretary at the Home Office, takes over in November. The handover comes mid-stream on several fronts the department is juggling at once: the Ofsted framework review ministers have asked for, the SEND reforms expected via the Education for All Bill, and Wednesday's Autumn Budget. Flag: no reporting yet on whether the change affects timelines for any of those. (Schools Week)
→ For your school: No direct action for schools, but worth knowing of the change if your trust deals directly with DfE officials on live consultations.

Jargon-buster
DSG override
Short for the Dedicated Schools Grant statutory override — an accounting rule that lets councils keep SEND overspends off their main balance sheet, rather than counting as a historic deficit against their reserves. Government has already written off around £5bn of deficits built up under it, and the OBR expects another £8.6bn to accrue before the override is due to end in 2028. What happens then, with councils' SEND deficits no longer ring-fenced, is one of the biggest unresolved questions hanging over this week's Autumn Budget.
