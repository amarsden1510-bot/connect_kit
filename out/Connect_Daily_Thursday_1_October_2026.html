■ Cyber-attacks on schools keep falling
▪ Ofqual's latest poll shows fewer, less damaging cyber incidents. The exam regulator's third annual Teacher Tapp survey, published today, found 27% of secondary schools and colleges were hit by a cyber incident in 2025/26, down from 29% the year before and 34% in 2023/24. Two-thirds of affected schools recovered immediately (up from 55%), and incidents causing critical damage fell to 7%. Ofqual still flags a responsibility gap: only 9% of staff say senior leadership owns cyber security, versus 46% pointing to IT teams. (GOV.UK / Ofqual)
→ For your school: Worth a line in the next SLT update confirming who in your school actually owns the cyber-incident response plan.
■ Sheffield/South Yorkshire: Barnsley's SEND place cost overrun
▪ Cost of 40 new SEND places in Barnsley rises by £4.4m. Barnsley Council's cabinet meets on 7 October to approve a revised £7.37m budget - up from the plans approved last October - to refurbish The Mill at Worsbrough Bridge into a 40-place specialist primary school. Survey and design work found more extensive access and equipment needs than expected; the scheme remains fully funded by a ring-fenced government capital grant, with Waterton Academy Trust as preferred delivery partner. (The Star)
→ For your school: A reminder that SEND capital schemes regularly cost more than first budgeted - useful context if you're advising on local place-planning timelines.
 
Jargon-buster - Ring-fenced grant
Comes up in the Barnsley SEND funding story above.
Government money that can legally only be spent on the purpose it was awarded for (e.g. new SEND places) - a council can't redirect it to plug other budget gaps, but equally can't flex it if costs rise, as Barnsley's are now doing.
