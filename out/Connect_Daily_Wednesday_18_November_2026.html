■ National policy
▪ Milburn's final NEET review calls for a legal 'Young People's Guarantee'. The long-delayed final report from Alan Milburn's review into youth inactivity proposes a legal duty on government to guarantee under-25s a route into work, education or training, modelled on local authorities' existing duties toward homeless people. It builds on his May interim report, which found young people with SEND are around 80% more likely to be NEET than their peers - a gap Milburn says needs addressing earlier than the post-16 transition point. Coverage cites a multi-billion-pound funding package attached to the plan, but the exact figure isn't yet corroborated across other reporting, so treat any number with caution until DfE confirms it. (The Big Issue)
→ For your school: Worth a look for anyone shaping post-16 transition or careers provision for SEND pupils.
■ Inclusion, SEND & alternative provision
▪ Ministers reportedly weighing concessions on SEND appeal rights. Amid continued backlash over plans to replace most EHCPs with Individual Support Plans - which, as proposed, would remove access to the independent SEND Tribunal for anyone below the top support tier - government sources suggest new appeal routes, potentially an ombudsman or independent review panel, are now under consideration. Campaigners are pushing for firm guarantees before the Education for All Bill, which isn't expected until early 2027. (Independent Schools Council daily news summary)
■ Behaviour, exclusions & attendance
▪ Exclusion reform call for evidence closes tomorrow. The DfE's call for evidence on behaviour management - covering internal suspension, reintegration support partnerships and schoolwork provision during the first days of a suspension or exclusion - closes 19 November. It feeds proposals in the 'Every Child Achieving and Thriving' white paper for a time-limited internal suspension framework. (GOV.UK)
