■ National pay & funding
▪ NEU calls off the 3 October ballot after the government's pension-savings offer. The union's national executive met yesterday and voted not to proceed with the formal strike ballot, after Powell wrote to NEU general secretary Daniel Kebede confirming schools can retain local savings from lower employer pension contributions — rather than having them reclaimed centrally — to help meet the 2026-27 pay award. The union says this addresses its 'fully funded' objection, though it wants the full national pension saving, which it puts at £3.3bn, to stay in education too; that total figure is as reported by the union and not independently verified here. (Tes)
→ For your school: Strike risk this term has lifted — worth confirming with your finance lead once written DfE guidance on using the saving is published.
▪ The saving comes from a Local Government Pension Scheme revaluation. A cut to employer contribution rates freed up roughly £500m across England's schools nationally; DfE says schools can keep it rather than have it clawed back by the Treasury. The pay ballot decision reached earlier this month did not extend to support staff. (LBC)
 
Jargon-buster - pension 'clawback'
Why yesterday's row was about accounting, not new cash.
When employer pension contribution rates fall, government normally reclaims ('claws back') the saving centrally. This time DfE let schools keep it — turning an accounting change into real money for pay bills.
